# Ownership on a lowered form

Status: built: the form, its lowering, the move and borrow passes on it, and escape, each reporting in the tree's place, and the measurement against `main`, which the cost section gives. The simplifications step 6 lists remain, each a cleanup with no change in what is refused. What a value lends, the places and the temporaries a borrow in it is of, is followed on the form, through the instructions that make the value (`formlends.go`), wherever a borrow can outlive its owner: what a `return` lets out, what a variable, a field or a binding keeps, what `Push`, `Insert`, `Extend` and a generic call store, what a value lent to a call or stored by a statement with several values holds, and what a deferred call reads, so that every shape of expression is seen the same way; step 5 says how. The expression is still read for what a closure captures, which its own form decides, for what a deferred call's saved view sees and a range loop holds over a value made here, and for whether a statement's value reads a variable it stores, each of them a rule on the construct rather than on a value's shape. This note says why the checker's move and borrow passes are to run on a form of each function in the order the program evaluates it, not on the syntax tree, what that form is, what each pass needs from it, and how the change lands with two programs newly refused and nothing else changed in what the language refuses. The rules are those of `04-borrows-ref-and-mut.md` and `10-borrow-checking.md`; this note moves where they are computed.

## Why a form

The checker types a body and checks its ownership in one walk over the syntax tree. The move and borrow passes record their effects, a read, a move, a borrow, a store, a drop, when the walk reaches the expression, so the order of the effects is the order of the walk. For most of the language the walk's order is the program's, and the passes are right. Where it is not, the checker needs a device for the construct: `carry` holds a value's loans across the stores of `a, b = b, a`; the write of an assignment was set aside until its value was checked, before the hold replaced it; and the place of an assignment now has every effect of its typing held in a list and made at the store, in order (`10-borrow-checking.md`, the graph). Each device covers one construct, and the edge of each was found by a program that read freed memory under the debug build and that the checker had accepted: `*p = eaten(p)`, `r, x := ref s, take(s)`, `pick(mut b, read(s)).n = taken(s)`, a closure or an `if` arm among a base call's arguments. Two stand today, accepted and unsafe, and this note's form refuses them: `*r += drained(v)` with `r := mut v[0]`, where the store's reach of `*r` is a use of `r` after the value moved `v`, which the borrow pass refuses as `cannot move v: v is borrowed by r at L:C, used at L:C`, as it refuses `*r = drained(v)`; and `xs[0] += takenXs(xs)` inside an `if` arm among a base call's arguments, where the store reaches `xs` again after the value moved it, which the move pass refuses as `use of moved value xs; moved at L:C`, as it does at the top level.

Two more things follow from checking on the tree. The rules of evaluation order live three times, in the checker and in each backend's walk, and once disagreed: the C emitter reached `first(mut bs)` before `grown(mut bs)` in `first(mut bs).n = grown(mut bs)`, where the checker, and the book, had the value first, so the checker proved the safety of a program other than the one compiled. And the next construct the language adds is checked in the wrong order until someone writes its device.

The language promises memory safety with no collector, and the checker is the whole of that promise: nothing at run time catches a use after free in a release build. A guarantee that depends on an enumeration of constructs is as complete as the last person to think about it.

## The decision

After a body is typed, the checker lowers it to a form of blocks and instructions in the order the program evaluates them, and runs the move pass and the borrow pass on that form. The form and its lowering live in `compiler/types`, exported, as the flow graph lives there today: `compiler/types` imports nothing above the syntax, and every package that reads a checked body, `compiler/emit/lower`, both backends, `compiler/doc`, `compiler/testrun`, `cmd/snello` and `lsp/server`, imports `compiler/types`, so the form is where all of them can read it, and moving the passes out of the checker to read a form elsewhere would move the `kept` flag on parameters, the error list and the server's integration for nothing the language checks differently. The devices go: `carry`, the hold and its list, `pin`, the regions, forks and floors of the move pass. The order of evaluation is stated once, in the lowering, and in a second stage, decided on its own below, the backends read it there too. Nothing changes in the source, in the messages or at run time: the form is the checker's, as its graph is today, and no program ever sees it.

This is the shape Rust's borrow checker took when it moved from the syntax tree to its mid-level IR, with the lifetimes it then computed on the control-flow graph, and the shape Swift's ownership and exclusivity checks have on SIL. Go lowers to SSA for its own analyses, liveness among them. Each exists because an analysis that needs the order of evaluation is written once over a form that has it, instead of once per construct over a tree that does not.

## The form

A body is a list of blocks; a block is a straight run of instructions; an edge joins blocks as control can go, the edges `10-borrow-checking.md` gives for each construct. An operand is a place, a variable and a path into it of field names and of `*`, the deref a `mut` borrow is reached through, `v`, `v.f`, `r.*.f`, or a temporary, a value the statement makes and drops at its end. Every instruction keeps the expression it comes from, for its position and its text in a message, `cannot move v: v is borrowed by r`, `use of moved value xs[0]`.

| Instruction | Meaning | Today's event |
|---|---|---|
| `read p` | the value at `p` is read: a Copy out of it, or a use of what it holds | use, read access |
| `move p` | the value at `p` leaves it; `p` and every place within it are moved until a store revives them | move, moved list |
| `borrow p ref` / `borrow p mut` → `t` | a borrow of `p` is made into the temporary `t`, copying the loans `p` holds at that moment, in the parts of `t` that reach them | loan, copy, ref or mut access |
| `make kind(t…)` → `t` | a literal, tuple, variant or closure is built from temporaries; each capture of a closure is a `read`, `borrow` or `move` before it, by its kind | loan, copy |
| `call f(t…)` → `t` | the callee runs with what the temporaries hold; what it lends is given back after; a closure passed lends its captures for the call | lend, lend captures |
| `store p ← t` | the temporary's value is given to the place, dropping what the place held, which a `Drop` reads as `drop` does; a borrow variable is pointed elsewhere, and a store through a `mut` ends what the place held | assignment, write access, rebind, through-ends |
| `store x <- t` of a temporary | a temporary outlives its statement, into a declared name or the block's value; a borrow of a temporary held is refused, `cannot keep a borrow of a temporary` | pin, carry, the temporary rule |
| `drop p` / `drop t` | the value is dropped; a type with a `Drop` reads what it borrows then | drop, use by a `Drop` |
| `defer f(t…)` | the arguments are evaluated now and lent to a holder live to every return; the call runs in each returning `leave`, before its drops; the whole-variable and view rules of a deferred call stay as rules on it | defer hold, defer view |
| `leave drops…` | a return, a `?` that returns, a `break` or `continue`: the deferred calls when it returns, the drops of what it leaves, then the edge | return, leave drops |
| `branch`, `join`, `loop`, `back` | the edges of the constructs, with a loop's head, its post block and its exit; a match guard that fails branches to the next arm, dropping what the pattern bound | blocks, guard fails |

A `borrow` into a temporary that dies in its statement is checked against the statement's later instructions at once, as the borrows lent for a call or statement are checked today, and is no loan of the solver; the solver runs for a body only when a `store` gives a borrow to a variable, a `leave` returns one, or a `borrow` copies loans, which is when the graph is solved today.

The lowering states, once, the order the book gives in its basics chapter and the checker applies today:

| Construct | Order |
|---|---|
| a call | the receiver is read, the arguments left to right, each `mut` argument lent in its place among them, the receiver lent last, after the arguments |
| an operator, a literal, a tuple | left to right |
| an assignment of one or several values | the indexes and keys of the places, left to right; the values, left to right; each store, where the rest of the place, `*p`, the struct a field is in, the call that gives it, is reached |
| a compound assignment | the place read, as the operator's left operand; the value; the place reached again and stored |
| `:=`, `var` with one or several values | the values, then the declarations and stores, left to right |
| `if`, `match`, `??`, `&&`, `\|\|` | the condition, the scrutinee or the left side, then the arm taken; a match that binds by value moves its value before the arms; a guard that fails goes on to the next arm |
| a `for` loop | the init, then each pass: the test, the body, the post statement, back to the test |
| `range` | the collection once: moved when an owned slice is ranged by value, lent when a borrow is ranged, lent `mut` for an iterator whose `Next` is called each pass; then each pass binds the item and runs the body |
| a loop over a type's parts | a loop over the parts, as the checker treats it today, with no `break` or `continue`; the backends write the body out once per part |
| `select` | each case's channel, and a send's value read, in order; then the case taken, which alone takes its value |
| `defer` | the arguments at the `defer`, the call where the function returns |
| `spawn`, a send | the arguments or the value, then the task or the channel takes them |
| a closure | a body of its own, lowered as a function, whose captures the `make` lists |

A generic body is lowered once, as it is checked once: an operand of a type parameter's type moves unless the parameter is bound by Copy, when it is read, as the checker decides today. For the second stage, where the backends write each instance, the same operand is a `read` in an instance whose argument is Copy, so an instruction over a type parameter's type is type-parametric and the emitter resolves it per instance.

A body's form is built into a slice of instructions and a slice of blocks kept with the checker and reused across bodies, as the flow graphs are kept today; a place is a small index into the body's variables or its temporaries, with its path, the field names, `*` and `[]` reached from it joined by dots; an interned path can replace the string when measured.

## The passes on the form

- **Moves** become a forward pass: the set of places that may be moved at each point, joined at a `join` by union, to a fixed point around a loop. A `read` or `borrow` of a place in the set is refused; a `store` to a place takes it and the places within it out. The sets are bitsets over the form's move sites, its `move` instructions of places that may be moved, numbered in order, as the liveness sets of a compiler's dataflow are bitsets over its variables: a block's sets are a few words, joined by or-ing them, and the site gives back the place, the position and the block for a message. Each block carries one set of the sites reached on this pass of every loop and one per loop of the sites come round it: a site's bit moves from the first to the loop's where the loop's back edge is taken, and stays there round the loops outside, so a use inside that loop is `moved in a previous iteration of the loop`, and a use outside it, or reached from the move on this pass, names the move's position. The forks, regions, floors and `moveBefore` of `move.go` go: a branch is blocks and edges.
- **Borrows** keep the three passes of `10-borrow-checking.md`, liveness backwards, loans in effect forwards, variables dropped forwards, over the form's blocks, with the instructions as the events. What a stored value lends and from where is read off its expression's shape and types, by the recorders the walk has today, `hold`, `derive`, `sources` and the captures of a closure, which need no order: the pass over the form calls them per instruction, in the form's order, resolving each name through the objects the typing recorded, since the scopes are closed once a body is typed. The rules of reading through a borrow stay as the loans-in-effect pass's rules on `borrow` and `store`: a `borrow` of a place that holds borrows copies them, and a `store` through a `mut` ends what the one place it points to held. `carry` goes: the value's `borrow` and `make` precede the `store`, so what the value read is held before the store takes effect, which is what `carry` arranged by hand. A `store`, `move` or `borrow mut` of a package variable is refused in this pass, as `access` refuses it today. Two things the form settles that the tree arranged by hand: a borrow made into a temporary, `t = borrow ref s`, is a holder of its own in the graph, assigned where it is made, lent the place, and used by each instruction that takes it, so that the loan is live along the paths to its last use, for a call's argument as for the collection a loop ranges over; and a read of a place whose value holds borrows takes what the place holds where it is read, into a holder the store of that value copies from, so that `a, b = b, a` gives each side what the other held before either store, which is what `carry` arranged. The messages of a loan lent for a call name no holder and no use, as the statement-scoped loans do today.
- **Escape** follows a borrow from its `borrow` to a `store` into a parameter's place or a `leave` that returns it, over the loans in effect at that instruction. Its rules on types and signatures, which types may hold a borrow and that a signature with two borrowed inputs and a borrowed result is refused, stay in typing.
- **Keep** stays in the typing walk: which parameters a function keeps is decided from what its body stores, returns, sends, spawns or passes to a kept parameter, as a fixed point over the package at its end, and nothing in it depends on the order of evaluation.
- **Send**, the checks of what crosses a task, and every check of types stay in the typing walk: they need no order.

What the passes refuse does not change, except for the two programs of the first section, which they refuse by the rules above with the messages given there. Every row of the refusals table in `10-borrow-checking.md` and every rule of `04-borrows-ref-and-mut.md` holds, with the same message at the same position. The test corpus is the proof: `testdata/errors`, the checker's tables, `TestFlowModel`, `TestFlowPaths`, which tests the graph edge by edge and takes the form's blocks, `TestBorrows`, `TestAssignOrder`, `TestSeveralValues`, and every program the reviews of the hold wrote, accepted and refused, run in debug on both backends. The two programs are `TestFormRefusals` from the start, each skipped with its reason until the pass that refuses it runs on the form, the move pass for the second and the borrow pass for the first, when it joins `TestAssignOrder` and `testdata/errors` as a refused row; the change is done when the corpus, those rows in it, passes.

## What it costs, and the bar

Measured on `main` before any of this, `BenchmarkCheck` in `compiler/types` checks the examples in 2.8 ms with 654 KB and 9,460 allocations; `BenchmarkCheck` in `lsp/server` checks the workspace in 73.7 ms, 2.1 ms per package, with 51.5 MB and 479,000 allocations. The checker runs in the language server on every keystroke, so the bar is the project's: no slower than `main` on either, allocations no higher, measured as the performance notes say, with interleaved rounds for rows within a percent, and with the parallel run of the landing plan off. The form is designed for it: one slice of instructions and one of blocks per body, reused; places as indexes; the solver's bit sets as today; the statement-scoped borrows checked at once and never solved; a body that gives no borrow to a variable, returns none and copies none lowered and left unsolved, as such a body is left unsolved now.

Measured with the form's passes reporting and the tree's gone, `BenchmarkCheck` in `compiler/types` given the record the compiler keeps, as every caller passes one, on the same idle machine in runs of six and five against `main`, the `cli-grep` example removed from both:

|---|---|---|
| `compiler/types` BenchmarkCheck, ms per op | 4.6 to 5.5 | 6.9 to 8.4 |
| `compiler/types` BenchmarkCheck, MB per op | 1.85 | 2.0 |
| `compiler/types` BenchmarkCheck, allocations per op | 10,900 | 11,200 |
| `lsp/server` BenchmarkCheck, ms per op | 90 to 104 | 113 to 131 |

Allocations are within one percent in the server and three in the checker, and at parity over a long run, where the library's one check is spread thin: 10,680 against 10,660 per op. Bytes are eight percent above in the checker and five in the server. Time is a quarter to two fifths behind, and the profile says where: the borrow pass over the form is twelve percent of the samples, eight of them the solver, the lowering seven, the move pass one and a half; on `main` the solver is below one percent, because a body in which no variable holds a borrow, returns none and copies none was never solved, its statement-scoped loans checked at once as they were made. On the form every borrow made into a temporary, a call's receiver among them, is a loan with a holder in the graph, so nearly every body is solved. The next performance item is the one this section planned: the loans of temporaries used within their block checked at once against the loans open there, and the solver run only for a body in which a variable holds a borrow, a borrow is returned or copied, or a loan crosses a block. What the section's bar asks is held for allocations and not yet for time; the language keeps the form, since what it decides is the order of evaluation and the tree could not, and the time is the next item on `main`.

For scale, Go's own type checker measured the same way on this machine, `go/types` checking its own package of 24,500 lines and `net/http` of 18,600, allocates 1,350 and 410 bytes per line, 10.1 and 3.6 objects per line, and takes 6.5 and 3.8 microseconds per line; the Snello checker over the examples' 2,800 lines, the move and borrow checks included, allocates about 715 bytes and 4.0 objects per line in about 2.5 microseconds. The rates are what to watch as programs grow: the scratch is reused from body to body, so the memory held is the package's tables and the largest body, and the work per line must stay flat on a large input, which the next round measures.

## The second stage: the backends

`07-lowering.md` declined a form between the tree and the backends on a premise this note changes: the checker did its analysis on the tree, and what the backends shared was `compiler/emit/lower`, rules over the checker's tables and no form, so a form only the LLVM backend used would have been a second statement of the rules the C emitter applied; it forecast a form growing in that package if a third backend came. The checker is that third reader, and the form grows in `compiler/types` for the reason given above. With the passes on it, the form is the one statement of evaluation order, and the emitters' own, `orderAssignment` in both over `lower.PlaceOperands`, and the hoisting of the C emitter's `hoist.go`, are the second and third. The emitters can keep their text and read the order from the form, placing each instruction's text where the form places it, or emit from the form outright. Either is a rewrite of both emitters' statement walks, to be decided with its own measurements, the time of a build with its baseline taken when the stage is designed, and the backend test corpus, whose outputs must not change. It is the step that makes a disagreement between the checker and the code impossible rather than unlikely, and it is not a condition of the first.

## Precedent

Rust checked borrows on its syntax tree until its non-lexical lifetimes, which it could only compute on a control-flow graph in evaluation order, its MIR; the move was made for precision and for the same ordering reasons as here. Swift runs its ownership checks on SIL, in ownership SSA form. Go has no borrow checker, and lowers to SSA for liveness and the analyses its backend needs. Snello takes the form and not the annotations: nothing is written in a program, where a borrow cannot be followed the program is refused with a message that says what to write, and the form is the checker's own, as its graph is today.
